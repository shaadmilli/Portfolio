# Data & Applied Economics Portfolio

Applied economics and data analysis projects spanning credit risk modeling,
econometrics, time series forecasting, and health economics. Each project
folder contains a full write-up (problem, approach, key findings, tools
used, and known limitations) alongside the underlying code.

Most projects here were built as coursework or independent study rather
than on the job — each README states its scope and data source plainly,
and limitations sections call out what a production version would need
to add (train/test splits, larger samples, causal identification checks,
etc.) rather than glossing over them.

## Projects

1. **[Lending Club Consumer Loan Pricing & Credit Risk Model](./lending-club-credit-risk)**
   Logistic regression on 1.3M closed consumer loans, paired with an
   Excel pricing framework that flags $43K+ in individually mispriced
   loans and shows only one of seven risk grades actually clears
   breakeven.

2. **[Education & Earned Income — A Regression Analysis Using CPS Data](./wage-regression-sas)**
   OLS regression on ~17K Census Population Survey observations testing
   whether education's effect on earnings holds up once demographic,
   health, and geographic controls — and CPS's own data imputation — are
   accounted for.

3. **[Retail Sales Forecasting — STL Decomposition & ETS Modeling](./retail-sales-forecasting)**
   12-month sales forecast for a small retailer, built by testing 60+
   specifications across six model families and picking the winner on
   accuracy *and* forecast plausibility, not accuracy alone.

4. **[Grocery Store Chain — Profitability Analytics](./gsc-power-bi-analytics)**
   Power BI dashboard decomposing a retail chain's profit across
   category, promotion, brand, store, and day of week — surfacing a
   promotion strategy that's quietly losing money on some campaigns.

5. **[Cost-Effectiveness Analysis of Alcohol Screening & Brief Intervention (ASBI)](./asbi-cea)**
   Health economics ICER/QALY analysis comparing two care-delivery
   models, showing one option is strictly dominated once long-term
   outcomes are priced in.
